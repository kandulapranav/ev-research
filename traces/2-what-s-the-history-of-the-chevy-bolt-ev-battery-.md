# What's the history of the Chevy Bolt EV battery fire recall? Are 2022-23 Bolts affected?

## 1. Gemini 3.5 Flash, no tools, no system prompt

The Chevrolet Bolt EV battery recall is one of the most significant and heavily publicized recalls in the history of electric vehicles. It involved nearly every Bolt ever produced up to late 2021, cost billions of dollars, and temporarily halted production of the vehicle. 

Here is the detailed history of the recall and how it affects the 2022 and 2023 model years.

---

### Part 1: History of the Chevy Bolt EV Recall

The recall unfolded in several phases between 2020 and 2022:

#### 1. The Early Warning Signs (Late 2020)
In late 2020, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) opened an investigation after receiving reports of several Chevy Bolt EVs catching fire. The fires seemed to originate in the backseat area while the cars were parked and charging. 
* **November 2020:** GM issued its first recall, covering approximately 69,000 Bolt EVs from the 2017–2019 model years.
* **The Interim Fix:** GM issued a software update that limited the battery's maximum charge to 90% to prevent the conditions that caused the fires.

#### 2. The Failed Software Fix and Escalation (Mid-2021)
In April 2021, GM believed it had found a permanent software diagnostic fix and restored the 100% charging capacity. However, in July 2021, a Bolt that had received the update caught fire. 
* **July 2021:** GM re-recalled the 2017–2019 Bolts and announced they would replace the battery modules.
* **August 2021 (The Massive Expansion):** GM expanded the recall to include **all remaining Bolt EVs and the newly released Bolt EUV**, spanning model years 2017 through 2022. This brought the total number of recalled vehicles to about 141,000.
* **The Advice to Owners:** GM advised owners to park their vehicles outside immediately after charging, not to charge them overnight indoors, and to maintain a minimum of 70 miles of remaining range at all times.

#### 3. Root Cause and Resolution (Late 2021)
GM and its battery supplier, LG Energy Solution, traced the fires to two rare manufacturing defects occurring in the *same* battery cell: a **torn anode tab** and a **folded separator**. When both defects were present in a single cell, it created a high risk of a short circuit and subsequent thermal runaway (fire).

* **The Cost:** LG agreed to pay GM **$1.9 billion** to cover the costs of the recall.
* **The Remedy:** 
  * For **2017–2019 models**: GM replaced the entire battery pack with a new, defect-free battery that also had an updated chemistry, increasing their range by about 8%. They also received an 8-year/100,000-mile battery warranty.
  * For **2020–2022 models**: GM installed advanced diagnostic software. If the software detected an anomaly, the battery was replaced. If no anomaly was found after 6,211 miles (10,000 km) of driving, the software restriction (which limited charge to 80%) was removed, restoring 100% charging capacity.

Production of the Bolt was halted for over six months and finally resumed in April 2022 after LG corrected its manufacturing processes.

---

### Part 2: Are 2022–2023 Bolts Affected?

The short answer is: **2022 models were initially affected, but 2023 models are completely unaffected.**

Here is the breakdown by year:

#### 2022 Bolt EV & EUV (Partially Affected)
* **Early-production 2022 models** (built before the production halt in late 2021) were included in the August 2021 recall. 
* **Late-production 2022 models** (built after April 2022, when production resumed) were built with defect-free batteries directly from the factory and were **not** subject to the recall.
* *How to check:* If you are buying a used 2022 Bolt, you can run its VIN through the NHTSA recall database. If it was an early-production model, it will either have had its battery replaced, or it will have the diagnostic software installed. 

#### 2023 Bolt EV & EUV (Not Affected)
* **2023 models are 100% unaffected.**
* By the time production of the 2023 model year began, LG had fully resolved the manufacturing defects at its factories. All 2023 Bolts were built with the safe, updated battery cells. 
* Because of the recall's resolution, the 2023 Chevy Bolt became one of the best-selling EVs in the U.S. due to a massive price cut GM introduced to win back customer trust.

### Summary for Buyers Today
If you are shopping for a used Chevy Bolt:
* **2017–2019 models** are highly desirable because almost all of them received a **brand-new battery pack** with a reset 8-year/100,000-mile warranty.
* **2020–2022 models** may have a new battery, or they may have diagnostic software installed that limits charging to 80% for the first 6,200 miles to ensure the battery is healthy before restoring 100% capacity.
* **2023 models** are completely safe, never had the recall defect, and have no charging limitations.

## The short answer
The Chevy Bolt EV has a history of high-voltage battery fire recalls spanning the 2017 to 2022 model years ([21V-560](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V560000)), ([21V-650](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V650000)). The 2022 Bolt EV and EUV models are affected by these battery recalls ([21V-650](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V650000)), ([21V650000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V650000)). In contrast, 2023 Bolt models are not affected by the battery fire recalls, with the 2023 Bolt EUV having zero total recalls ([2022](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EUV&modelYear=2022)) and the 2023 Bolt EV only being recalled for non-battery hazards such as seat belt pretensioner fires ([22V930000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=22V930000)), ([23V845000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=23V845000)).

## Recalls
Initial battery fire recalls began with campaign 20V-701 for 2017-2019 Bolt EVs and were superseded by 21V-560, which required parking outside, limiting charging, and ultimately replacing defective battery modules ([20V-701](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=20V701000)), ([21V-560](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V560000)). Recall 21V-650 expanded the battery fire campaign to 2020-2022 Bolt EVs and EUVs, implementing interim 90% charge limits and replacing defective modules ([21V-650](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V650000)), ([21V650000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V650000)). Subsequent software-focused recalls, 24V-481 and 24V-812, addressed cases where advanced diagnostic software failed to install correctly on 2020-2022 models ([24V-481](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V481000)), ([24V-812](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V812000)), ([24V481000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V481000)), ([24V812000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V812000)). All of these battery and diagnostic recall repairs require dealer visits, as zero over-the-air fixes were reported ([2022](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EUV&modelYear=2022)), ([2023 recalls](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EV&modelYear=2023)).

## What owners report
Across the 2017–2023 model years, NHTSA recorded 21 fires, 38 crashes, and 19 injuries for the Chevrolet Bolt EV ([2017 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EV&modelYear=2017)). Owners submitted 339 complaints containing battery, fire, smoke, or thermal keywords, with 21 complaints filed for 2022 models and 4 for 2023 models ([2017 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EV&modelYear=2017)), ([2022 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EV&modelYear=2022)), ([2023 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=CHEVROLET&model=BOLT+EV&modelYear=2023)). Many owners reported that the recall diagnostic software flagged battery cell failures and triggered diagnostic codes, causing reduced propulsion or a refusal to move ([ODI 11736147](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11736147)), ([ODI 11748422](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11748422)). Other complaints note charging failures and disputes where GM used a 6,213-mile tracking window to deny coverage for hardware issues ([ODI 11732425](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11732425)), ([ODI 11745256](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11745256)).

## Investigations & crash tests
NHTSA opened Preliminary Evaluation PE20016 to investigate battery fires on 2017-2020 Bolt EVs, which closed in 2022 after leading to the initial battery recall ([PE20016](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE20016)). Another investigation, PE24028, examined thermal events associated with using Enel X Way JuiceBox home chargers on 2018 models but closed in 2025 without a recall ([PE24028](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE24028)). Defect Petition DP22005 investigated pedestrian alert sounds on 2017-2022 models, resulting in recall 22V063000 ([DP22005](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=DP22005)). The provided findings do not contain any information regarding vehicle crash tests.

## Range & specs by model year
* **2017-2019**: Deliver an EPA range of 238 miles and have a 240V charge time of 9.3 hours ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml)).
* **2020-2021**: Deliver an increased EPA range of 259 miles, but the 240V charge time rises to 10.0 hours ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/42191.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/42191.shtml)).
* **2022-2023**: Maintain the 259-mile EPA range, while the 240V charge time improves significantly to 7.5 hours ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43955.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43955.shtml)).
* **Trims**: Across all model years, the Bolt EV was sold only in a single trim level per year ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml)).

## What this data can't tell you
The provided findings do not contain information regarding battery cell chemistry, capacity, or manufacturing differences, such as the Ochang versus Holland plants ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/38187.shtml)). This data also lacks specific details regarding the exact role, mechanics, or mileage limits of GM's 6,213-mile tracking window used to monitor battery health and determine recall coverage ([ODI 11745256](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11745256)). Finally, crash test safety ratings are not included in the provided findings.
