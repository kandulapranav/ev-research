# Hyundai Ioniq 5: which model years should I avoid buying used?

## 1. Gemini 3.5 Flash, no tools, no system prompt

The Hyundai Ioniq 5 is highly regarded as one of the best electric vehicles on the market, but because it was a brand-new platform launched for the **2022 model year**, it has experienced some notable "first-generation" growing pains. 

If you are shopping for a used Ioniq 5, the model year you should be most cautious about—and potentially avoid unless specific conditions are met—is the **2022 model year**. 

Here is a breakdown of why, along with what to look out for in other model years.

---

### 1. 2022 Hyundai Ioniq 5 (Avoid or Proceed with High Caution)
As the debut model year, the 2022 Ioniq 5 carries the highest risk of mechanical and software issues. 

**Major Reasons to Avoid/Be Cautious:**
*   **The ICCU Failure (The Big One):** The Integrated Charging Control Unit (ICCU) is a notorious point of failure for 2022 (and early 2023) models. When it fails, it can cause the 12-volt battery to die, put the car into "limp mode" (loss of power while driving), or brick the car entirely. While Hyundai issued a massive recall to update/replace this, some used cars may not have had the service done.
*   **Lack of Battery Preconditioning:** Early 2022 models (especially RWD trims) did not come with manual battery preconditioning. Without this, DC fast-charging in cold weather is incredibly slow. While Hyundai eventually released a dealer-installed software update for this, not all used 2022 cars received it.
*   **12V Battery Drain Issues:** The 12-volt accessory battery in the 2022 model is prone to dying unexpectedly, often related to the ICCU issue or frequent third-party app polling (like BlueLink).

*Verdict:* **Avoid early 2022 RWD models.** If you do buy a 2022 model, ensure it is an AWD version (which had preconditioning earlier) and verify through the VIN that all ICCU recalls have been completed.

---

### 2. 2023 Hyundai Ioniq 5 (Proceed with Caution)
The 2023 model year resolved some of the software headaches of the 2022, but still carries some hardware risks.

**Key Issues to Watch For:**
*   **ICCU Recall:** 2023 models are still heavily affected by the ICCU recall. 
*   **Level 2 Charging Port Overheating:** Many 2022 and 2023 owners reported that when charging at home on a high-amp (48A) Level 2 charger, the charge port would overheat, causing the car to stop charging. Hyundai’s "fix" was a software update that simply throttles the charging speed down, rather than fixing the physical overheating.

*Verdict:* **A decent buy, but check service history.** Ensure the ICCU recall was completed. If you plan to charge at home at 48 amps, be prepared for potential charging slowdowns.

---

### 3. 2024 Hyundai Ioniq 5 (The Recommended Used Choice)
By 2024, Hyundai had ironed out the vast majority of the launch issues. 

**Why it’s a safer buy:**
*   **Updated Hardware:** The ICCU issues were largely addressed at the factory.
*   **Added Safety:** Hyundai added rear-side airbags and reinforced the B-pillars for better crash safety.
*   **WiFi Capability:** Introduction of wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto (via software update).

*Verdict:* **Highly Recommended.** This is the safest bet for a used Ioniq 5.

---

### General Ioniq 5 "Quirks" to Know (All Years 2022–2024)
If you buy any used Ioniq 5 from these years, you must live with two widely criticized design choices:
1.  **No Rear Wiper:** 2022, 2023, and 2024 models do not have a rear windshield wiper. The rear window gets incredibly dirty in rain or snow, severely limiting rear visibility. (Hyundai finally added a rear wiper to the refreshed **2025** model).
2.  **No Wireless Apple CarPlay/Android Auto (Initially):** Depending on the exact software version installed by the previous owner, you may still have to plug your phone in via USB-A (not USB-C) to use phone projection.

---

### Used Buyer’s Checklist
If you find a great deal on a used 2022 or 2023 Ioniq 5, do not buy it unless you verify the following:

1.  **The VIN Recall Check:** Run the VIN through the NHTSA recall lookup tool. Ensure **Recall 257** (the ICCU software update/replacement) has been completed.
2.  **The Warranty Difference:** Note that Hyundai’s famous 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain warranty **does not** fully transfer to the second owner. It drops to a **5-year/60,000-mile** warranty. However, the **10-year/100,000-mile EV Battery/Hybrid System warranty *does* transfer** to subsequent owners in the US.
3.  **Buy Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) if possible:** Buying a CPO Hyundai restores the 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain warranty, which is highly recommended for peace of mind.

## 2. EV Research

VERDICT: Buy with caution | Best years: 2025 | Avoid: 2022, 2023, 2024

## The short answer
The 2022–2024 models face a major safety recall for a faulty Integrated Charging Control Unit (ICCU) that can cause a sudden loss of drive power ([24V868000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V868000)). The 2025 model year offers significantly improved driving range but currently has the highest number of safety recalls at nine ([2025](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2025)). Additionally, some 2025 owners still report experiencing identical ICCU failures ([ODI 11773883](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11773883)). Except for a single over-the-air update, all safety recall fixes on these vehicles require a physical dealer visit ([25V235000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V235000)).

## Recalls
A major recall affecting the 2022–2024 model years covers 145,642 vehicles to fix an ICCU issue that stops charging the 12-volt battery ([24V868000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V868000)). The 2025 model year is affected by nine safety recalls, including an electrical short and fire risk from an improperly tightened high voltage battery bus bar in 10 vehicles ([2025](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2025)), ([25V482000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V482000)). Other recalls include a shifter control software error in 10,729 vehicles for 2022 ([22V324000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=22V324000)), and an improperly heat-treated inner driveshaft for 1,165 vehicles in the 2023 model year ([24V065000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24V065000)). Aside from one over-the-air software update for Ioniq 5 N braking and acceleration issues, all safety recall fixes must be performed by a dealership ([25V235000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V235000)).

## What owners report
The electrical system is the most common source of owner complaints, representing 73.7% of all 1,339 filed reports across the 2022–2025 model years ([2022 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2022)). The ICCU is a primary problem with 769 complaints, often stopping 12-volt battery charging and leaving vehicles bricked ([2022 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2022)), ([ODI 11768588](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11768588)). Owners also report a total of 233 complaints for 12V auxiliary battery failures, which can cause flickering lights and render the vehicle undrivable (2022 complaints), ([ODI 11757298](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11757298)). Additionally, there are 126 reports of sudden loss of power, which is sometimes preceded by a loud pop noise from under the rear seat ([2022 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2022)), ([ODI 11762941](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11762941)).

## Investigations & crash tests
The NHTSA opened investigation PE23011 to evaluate sudden loss of motive power caused by ICCU failures on 2022–2024 models, which led directly to the recall ([PE23011](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE23011)). Another investigation, PE24028, evaluated residential charger electrical arcing and potential fires for the 2022 model year and was closed in June 2025 ([PE24028](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE24028)). Defect Petition DP22005 evaluated pedestrian alert sounds in 2022 models and prompted recall 22V063000 ([DP22005](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=DP22005)). There are currently no open NHTSA defect investigations or ICCU recalls listed for the 2025 model year ([PE23011](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE23011)), ([25V064000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V064000)). No crash tests are detailed in the provided safety records, which focus entirely on the closed defect investigations ([PE23011](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE23011)), ([PE24028](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE24028)), ([DP22005](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=DP22005)).

## Range & specs by model year
The 2022 and 2024 model years share similar specs, with the base Standard Range RWD offering 220 miles of range and the Long Range RWD reaching up to 303 miles ([44924](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44924.shtml)), ([46960](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/46960.shtml)). For the 2025 model year, driving range increased to 245 miles for the base Standard Range and 318 miles for the longest-range RWD trim ([48714](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/48714.shtml)), ([48713](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/48713.shtml)). The 2025 model year also expanded its trim options to include the AWD XRT with a 259-mile range and the high-performance Ioniq 5 N with a 221-mile range ([48712](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/48712.shtml)), ([48360](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/48360.shtml)). Charging a 2022 base Standard Range RWD using a 240V source takes 6.3 hours, while the Long Range RWD takes 8.5 hours ([44924](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44924.shtml)), ([44923](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44923.shtml)).

## What this data can't tell you
Although the fuel economy findings outline range and charge times, they do not provide the actual battery capacities in kWh for any of the model years ([44924](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44924.shtml)), ([48713](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/48713.shtml)). The available records cannot tell you whether the 2025 model will eventually face an official ICCU recall, even though owners have reported identical electrical failures ([25V064000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V064000)), ([ODI 11773883](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11773883)). Furthermore, the safety documents do not include crash test ratings from the NHTSA to evaluate occupant protection, focusing instead entirely on mechanical recalls and defect investigations ([PE23011](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE23011)), ([25V064000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V064000)). The complaints also cannot tell you the actual failure rate of the ICCU, as complaints are unverified reports and are not divided by overall vehicle sales ([2022 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=HYUNDAI&model=IONIQ+5&modelYear=2022)).
