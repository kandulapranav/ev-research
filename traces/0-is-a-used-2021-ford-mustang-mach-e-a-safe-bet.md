# Is a used 2021 Ford Mustang Mach-E a safe bet?

## 1. Gemini 3.5 Flash, no tools, no system prompt

A used 2021 Ford Mustang Mach-E is a **highly appealing EV, but it is not a "blind" safe bet.** Because 2021 was the launch year for the Mach-E, it suffered from some first-year teething issues. 

However, because used EV prices have plummeted, you can now buy a Mach-E for a fraction of its original MSRP—often qualifying for the **$4,000 U.S. federal used EV tax credit**. 

If you do your homework and buy one with a documented service history, it can be an excellent, safe, and reliable purchase. Here is a breakdown of what makes it a good bet, the risks to watch out for, and a pre-purchase checklist.

---

### The Good: Why it is a Safe Bet

*   **Excellent Safety Ratings:** If you mean "safe" in a crash, yes, it is incredibly safe. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) named the 2021 Mach-E a **Top Safety Pick**, earning the highest "Good" rating in all crashworthiness tests.
*   **Transferable Warranty:** Ford’s high-voltage battery and electric drivetrain components are covered for **8 years or 100,000 miles** (whichever comes first). Since it’s a 2021, you will have plenty of factory warranty left to cover the most expensive components.
*   **Over-the-Air (OTA) Updates:** Unlike older traditional cars, Ford continuously updates the Mach-E's software. A 2021 model today has better range management, a revised user interface, and faster charging than it did when it was brand new.
*   **Great Value:** Depreciation has hit EVs hard. You can easily find Premium or California Route 1 trims (originally $50k+) for under $25,000, making them eligible for the $4,000 used EV tax credit (if buying from a dealer in the US).

---

### The Risks: Why it Might NOT Be a Safe Bet

Because 2021 was the first model year, there are several known issues you must navigate:

#### 1. The HVBJB Recall (The Big One)
The High-Voltage Battery Junction Box (HVBJB) on 2021 and early 2022 models was prone to overheating, especially during DC fast-charging or hard acceleration. This could cause the car to lose power or fail to start. 
*   **The Fix:** Ford issued a major safety recall. Ensure the vehicle has had the physical HVBJB replacement completed by a Ford dealer, not just the temporary software patch.

#### 2. Windshield and Panoramic Roof Recall
Early 2021 models had issues with urethane adhesion on the windshield and the panoramic glass roof, meaning they could potentially pop loose in a crash. 
*   **The Fix:** Ford recalled these to reseal the glass. Ensure this has been done.

#### 3. 12-Volt Battery Drain
Early Mach-Es had a software bug where the traditional 12-volt battery (which powers the lights and computer systems) would drain while the car was plugged in to charge the main battery. 
*   **The Fix:** Software updates have largely resolved this, but some owners had to have their 12V battery replaced.

#### 4. Firm/Bouncy Ride Quality
The suspension on the 2021 non-GT models is known for being somewhat "bouncy" or stiff over uneven pavement. Ford tweaked the suspension in later years, but the 2021 can feel a bit unsettled on rough roads.

---

### Which Trim to Choose?
*   **Avoid the Base "Select" Trim (if you want range):** The Standard Range battery gets about 211–230 miles. 
*   **Target the Premium or California Route 1 (Extended Range):** These feature the larger battery, offering **270 to 300 miles of range**. 
*   **AWD vs. RWD:** AWD models are faster and better in snow but lose about 20–30 miles of range compared to RWD models.

---

### Your Checklist Before Buying a Used 2021 Mach-E

If you are shopping for one, do not buy until you check off these three things:

1.  **Run the VIN for Recalls:** Go to the NHTSA recall website. Make sure the **HVBJB recall (Recall 23V-865 or 22V-412)** and the **glass roof/windshield recalls** have been completed.
2.  **Check the Battery Health:** Ask a Ford dealer (or use an OBD2 scanner with the Car Scanner app) to check the High-Voltage Battery State of Health (SoH). Most Mach-Es show very little degradation (usually 92% to 98% health after 3 years), but it's good to verify.
3.  **Confirm Tax Credit Eligibility:** If you are buying in the US and the car is under $25,000, make sure the dealership is registered with the IRS so they can apply the $4,000 used EV tax credit directly at the point of sale.

### The Verdict
If you buy a 2021 Mach-E **with completed recalls and a clean battery health report**, it is a **very safe bet** and one of the best values on the used car market today. If the dealer or seller cannot prove the recalls have been performed, walk away.

## The short answer
The 2021 Ford Mustang Mach-E has a high number of recalls and owner complaints, particularly concerning its electrical system and high-voltage battery ([2021 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH-E&modelYear=2021), [2021](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH+E&modelYear=2021)). Many owners report sudden power loss while driving, and there is an active federal safety investigation into collisions involving the BlueCruise driving assistance system ([11767486](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11767486), [EA25001](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=EA25001)). While the vehicle offers competitive range and electric performance, potential buyers should check if all major recalls have been correctly completed on the vehicle they are considering ([23V687000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=23V687000), [25V130000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V130000)).

## Recalls
The 2021 model year has 13 recorded recalls, 12 of which require dealer visits ([2021](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH+E&modelYear=2021), [22V333000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=22V333000)). Multiple major recalls address overheating high-voltage battery main contactors, which can cause sudden loss of drive power and required replacing the battery junction box ([22V412000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=22V412000), [23V687000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=23V687000)). Some of these vehicles were previously repaired incorrectly under the initial battery recall, prompting a follow-up recall to correct them ([25V130000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=25V130000)). Other recalls involve rear differential pinion shaft fractures, potential windshield and panoramic roof glass detachment, and improperly tightened front subframe bolts ([26V417000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=26V417000), [21V711000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V711000), [21V712000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V712000), [21V140000](https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=21V140000)).

## What owners report
Owners have filed 353 complaints with the NHTSA, with the electrical system accounting for 42.2% of the reports ([2021 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH-E&modelYear=2021)). There are 97 complaints specifically mentioning battery, charging, or power loss, with owners reporting sudden "Stop Safely Now" alerts and immediate power loss while accelerating or driving ([2021 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH-E&modelYear=2021), [11767349](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11767349), [11719257](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11719257)). Other frequent complaints involve forward collision avoidance warnings, adaptive cruise control, and failures to accept charges at public fast-charging stations (2021 complaints, [11761335](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/odinumber?odinumber=11761335)). Combined national and Washington State registration data yields a complaint rate of 391.8 per 1,000 registered vehicles for the 2021 model year ([2021 complaints](https://api.nhtsa.gov/complaints/complaintsByVehicle?make=FORD&model=MUSTANG+MACH-E&modelYear=2021)).

## Investigations & crash tests
The NHTSA currently has an active Engineering Analysis, EA25001, investigating collisions involving the Ford BlueCruise system in 2021-2024 model years ([EA25001](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=EA25001)). This investigation was opened on January 17, 2025, following a preliminary evaluation after reports of two fatal collisions involving BlueCruise-equipped Mustang Mach-E vehicles ([EA25001](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=EA25001), [PE24012](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=PE24012)). Previous investigations into pedestrian alert sounds and high-voltage battery contactor failures have been closed after leading to recalls ([DP22005](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=DP22005), [RQ23004](https://www.nhtsa.gov/?nhtsaId=RQ23004)). The provided findings do not contain any crash test ratings for the vehicle.

## Range & specs by model year
The base 2021 Mustang Mach-E RWD trim has an EPA-estimated range of 230 miles and an efficiency of 33.86 kWh/100 mi ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43604.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43604.shtml)). The longest-range RWD California Route 1 trim offers 305 miles of range with an efficiency of 33.34 kWh/100 mi ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43683.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43683.shtml)). Drivetrain options across the 2021 lineup include RWD and AWD configurations, with power outputs ranging from 198 kW to 216 kW, and dual 179 kW motors in the GT models ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43604.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/43604.shtml), [https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44797.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44797.shtml)). The performance-focused GT Performance trim has the lowest efficiency at 41.19 kWh/100 mi and a reduced range of 260 miles ([https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44798.shtml](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/noframes/44798.shtml)). The provided data does not contain specifications for any model years other than 2021.
